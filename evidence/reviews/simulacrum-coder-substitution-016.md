# Simulacrum review — Coder substitution after GLM quota exhaustion

## Frame submitted

GLM-5.3 authored substantial uncommitted Guildhall product code, its Ollama quota is exhausted, and the implementation-blind Claude Tester is complete. The proposed alternative was a recorded routing amendment to a fresh GPT-5.6 Coder in the same isolated lane and state, rather than correlating the Coder with Claude or purchasing unapproved usage.

## Disposition

Simulacrum rejected the binary framing and required the proof claim to be named first:

1. A claim that GLM-5.3 alone can produce the artifact is destroyed by substitution.
2. A claim that an author independent of the Tester can produce the artifact survives a GPT substitution, but the implementation becomes explicitly composite.
3. A claim about the declared pipeline survives only if mid-run author substitution is already part of that pipeline; otherwise the measured system changed.

It further identified quota exhaustion as a result rather than mere inconvenience if feasibility within the configured budget/session is part of the claim. Recording the substitution is insufficient unless the changed attribution propagates into every downstream claim. Its operational rule was: if substitution is authorized for claim 2 or an already-declared form of claim 3, every artifact and summary must say “composite author, GLM-5.3 to GPT-5.6, quota exhaustion”; it must never be reported as a single Coder result.

## Validator application

Guildhall's outcome claim is about a functioning three-store knowledge system and its brownfield effect, not GLM capability. However, frozen `spec/source-request.md` SRC-6 expressly names GLM-5.3 as the Coder. Mid-run model substitution is not declared in the ratified pipeline. Therefore a different Coder requires a founder-ratified routing amendment; Agy can block or decline to block but cannot supply that authority.

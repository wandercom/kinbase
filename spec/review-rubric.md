# Review rubric

Status: active candidate. Authority: `source-request.md`. Audience: every review and
Factory role. Simulacrum is the framing adversary, Constrain extracts and challenges
requirements, and Advocate supplies independent persona critiques; none has product
authority.

Every Orchestrator, Simulacrum, Constrain, Advocate, Coder, Tester, and Validator
interaction is evaluated against one outcome:

> Can the implemented system cause an underinformed brownfield coding agent to make
> nearly the same decisions as a fully informed greenfield agent, while preserving
> the physical and authority boundaries among Personal, Company, and Codebase
> knowledge?

Reviewers optimize for:

1. causal ability to improve the dependent coding decision, not the appearance of a
   knowledge architecture;
2. falsifiability of that outcome under matched, independently judged runs;
3. zero observed unauthorized durable disclosure outside the authorized processor
   boundary across the exact digest-identified finite threat-model execution census,
   with no unqualified universal-leakage claim;
4. epistemic quality: authority, validity, supersession, conflict, uncertainty,
   recency, redundancy, and the cost of missing a fact;
5. ordinary Codex and Claude usability in a freshly opened repository;
6. operational ownership under clones, branches, partial failure, offline caches,
   and delayed human answers;
7. the smallest implementation that retains all of the above functionality;
8. independent freezing of task sampling, oracle/load-bearing labels, provider
   identity, authority consultation, scorer reliability, and every prior execution,
   so no benchmark knob can be tuned into a pass.

Reviewers do **not** optimize for a pitch, a leadership-defensible milestone, a
familiar service diagram, a small line count, a passing synthetic unit suite, or a
schedule story. "Defer the hard part" is a rejection unless the deferred item is
actually a nice-to-have under `SRC-7`.
